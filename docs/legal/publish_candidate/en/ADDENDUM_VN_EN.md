## Article 2 (Cross-border Transfer — TIA Filing Obligation)

1. Cross-border transfer of Vietnamese user data to Korean servers occurs continuously from the point of collection. Transfer requirements: (i) valid consent including the fact of cross-border transfer, the recipient country, and the recipient, (ii) filing of Transfer Impact Assessment (TIA) documents with A05 (the Cyber Security Department of the Ministry of Public Security) within 60 days from the commencement of processing, (iii) renewal upon changes (every 6 months), (iv) acceptance of authority inspections. A DPIA shall also be filed within 60 days from the commencement of processing.
2. Standard wording of the transfer agreement (specifying the purpose, data subjects, data items, period, legal basis, and protection responsibilities) shall be maintained as an annex.
3. Under the Data Law (60/2024) · Decision 20/2025, once the personal data threshold is reached, the transfer may be escalated to being subject to approval/notification.

## Article 3 (Expansion of Data Subject Rights)

1. The right to withdraw consent and the right to request deletion under the PDPL are guaranteed, and upon withdrawal the principle is to cease processing and delete.
2. Consent must be specific and explicit for each purpose (in writing or by a verifiable electronic method; silence/non-response does not constitute consent), and consent for unrelated purposes shall not be made a condition of service provision.

## Article 4 (Legal Basis for Purpose ② Anonymized/Aggregated Statistics)

1. Purpose ② includes the anonymization/statistical purpose within the notified purposes, but because a commercial resale purpose is combined therewith, it is separately specified in the notice. This is based on the analysis that fully de-identified outputs fall outside the scope of personal information under the PDPL; however, the boundary between anonymous vs. pseudonymous and the criteria for re-identification determination are subject to source confirmation.
2. Purposes ③④⑤ are maintained as opt-in (default OFF) — this is consistent with Vietnam's principle of explicit consent for each purpose.

## Article 5 (Marketing · Email)

1. Advertising emails·text messages·phone calls are defined as spam under Decree 91/2020 without prior explicit consent (opt-in), and a B2B exception has not been confirmed. Initial outreach in Vietnam is limited to a structure of (i) based on prior touchpoints such as exhibitions·business cards, (ii) via partners/agents, (iii) one-time contact to a corporate general representative address followed by conversion to opt-in.
2. When sending, compliance with advertising markers (labeling)·immediate opt-out guarantee·prohibition of sending from DNC-registered numbers is observed.

## Article 6 (Deletion · Notification Deadlines)

Upon withdrawal, personal information is deleted, and only aggregated outputs for which irreversible anonymization has been completed persist (separating the wording "upon withdrawal, delete personal information + only anonymized aggregate persists"). The impossibility of retroactive recovery of already-provided portions is limited to anonymized/aggregated outputs. The rationale for long-term retention (the need for long-term time series of industry statistics) is specified in the notice, and the specific period is managed under the internal data retention policy.

## Article 7 (Governing Law · Disputes)

Notwithstanding the governing-law designation of the Master Terms, protections conferred by mandatory provisions such as Vietnamese consumer·e-commerce law are not excluded.

## Article 8 (Language)

For documents submitted to authorities such as TIA/DPIA, Vietnamese-language requirements are anticipated, and consent screens·notices are provided in a Vietnamese-language version as a principle (from the standpoint of fulfilling verifiable electronic consent requirements).
